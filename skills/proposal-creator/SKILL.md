# Proposal Creator

Turns an already-scoped opportunity into a structured proposal document a prospect can read, understand, and sign off on without a follow-up call to decode it.

## When to use

Use this after an opportunity has been qualified and scoped — not to figure out whether or how to pursue it. If the problem, rough approach, or budget range isn't yet known, that's a scoping gap to close first, not something this skill should guess at.

## Inputs needed

Before drafting, confirm:
- **The problem** — what the prospect is trying to solve or achieve, in their own words where possible
- **The proposed approach** — what will actually be done, at a level a non-specialist can follow
- **Scope boundaries** — what's included and, just as important, what's explicitly not included
- **Timeline** — key milestones or a start/end window
- **Investment** — pricing, or the pricing structure if a number isn't set yet
- **Point of contact / next step** — who signs off and what happens after they say yes

If any of these is missing, ask for it rather than inventing a placeholder — a proposal with a fabricated timeline or price is worse than no proposal.

## Structure

Write the proposal in this order. Keep every section skimmable — assume the reader will scan headers before reading prose.

1. **Situation** — one short paragraph restating the problem in the prospect's terms, so they immediately recognize themselves in it.
2. **Proposed approach** — what will be done and why this approach, not an exhaustive methodology dump.
3. **Scope & deliverables** — a concrete, bulleted list of what's included. Note exclusions explicitly if the engagement has an obvious boundary a prospect might assume is covered.
4. **Timeline** — milestones or phases, not just a single end date.
5. **Investment** — the price or pricing structure, stated plainly. Don't bury it or make the reader hunt for it.
6. **Next steps** — the exact action that moves this from proposal to signed engagement, and who owns it.

## Rules

- Mirror the prospect's own language for the problem — don't reframe it into house jargon they didn't use.
- Every deliverable in scope must be concrete enough that "is this done?" has an unambiguous answer.
- State the price. A proposal that dodges the number isn't a proposal, it's a teaser.
- Keep it as short as the opportunity allows — a small engagement doesn't need a ten-section document.

## Output format

A complete proposal document following the structure above, ready to send with no placeholder text — if a section can't be filled in from the inputs given, flag that gap instead of guessing.
