# Long-Form

Drafts a full-length article — blog post, guide, or case study — from a brief and outline, producing the primary piece of content the rest of the pipeline can repurpose or link back to.

## When to use

Use this once an idea has cleared [[idea-review]] (and, if there were competing options, [[idea-tournament]]) and a brief with an outline exists. This is the drafting step, not the ideation or scoping step — if there's no brief or outline yet, get one before drafting. Typically fed by [[researcher]] for sourced facts, data, or quotes when the piece needs them. This produces the source piece that [[repurpose]] later derives channel-specific content from — don't use this skill to write a newsletter (use [[newsletter-writer]]) or a short-form post directly.

## Inputs needed

Before drafting, confirm:
- **Brief** — topic, angle, target reader, and the goal of the piece (educate, rank, convert, etc.)
- **Outline** — section-level structure, even rough; don't invent the shape of the argument from scratch
- **Source material** — research, data, quotes, or examples to draw on, if the piece needs to cite anything beyond argument from first principles
- **Target length** — rough word count or a comparable published piece to match
- **Voice/style reference**, if one exists for this house or publication

If the outline is missing or too thin to draft from, flag the gap rather than inventing structure wholesale.

## Structural conventions

Long-form is written for a reader who arrived via search or a link, not an inbox — structure for depth and standalone comprehension, not scannability under time pressure.

- **Lead** — open with the concrete problem or claim, not a throat-clearing preamble. The reader should know what this piece is about in the first two sentences.
- **Body sections** — follow the outline's structure; each section should stand on its own with a clear point, not just transition prose into the next one.
- **Evidence** — back claims with the source material provided; don't fabricate statistics, quotes, or examples not present in the inputs.
- **Headers** — use real, specific headers a reader could scan to find a section again, not generic labels like "Overview" or "More thoughts."
- **Conclusion** — land on the piece's actual point, not a generic summary restating each section title.
- **No inbox-format tics** — no "P.S.", no subject-line-style hooks, no section teasers written for an open rate. That's newsletter-writer's job, not this one.

## Output format

A complete draft article in the target voice, following the outline's structure, with headers, ready for editorial review. Flag any section where the source material didn't cover what the outline called for, rather than filling the gap with invented content.
