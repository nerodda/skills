# Researcher

Pulls sourced, cited research on a fixed topic and returns a research brief a writer can draft directly from, so claims in the finished piece are backed by real sources instead of invented on the fly.

## When to use

Use after an idea has passed [[idea-review]] (or won an [[idea-tournament]]) and before drafting starts in [[long-form]], [[newsletter-writer]], [[launch-video]], [[youtube-script]], [[webinar-kit]], or [[lead-magnet-creator]] — anywhere the draft will lean on outside facts, data, or quotes. Skip it for pieces that are pure opinion/POV with no external claims to back. Not a substitute for trend-scanning skills like [[linkedin-trend-scan]], [[x-trend-scan]], or [[web-search-scan]] — those watch what's current; this digs into one fixed topic in depth.

## Inputs needed

- **Topic/angle** and the specific claims or questions that need answering
- **Target piece and channel** — blog, newsletter, video script, webinar — since citation depth and tone differ
- **Depth required** — quick grounding (3–5 sources) vs. a comprehensive brief (10+)
- **Existing sources or claims already in hand** that need verifying, separate from new sourcing
- **Recency requirement** — needs current data vs. evergreen is fine

## Process

- Primary sources over secondary: an original study, dataset, or first-party report beats someone's summary of it.
- Every claim carries its source inline next to it — not a bibliography dump at the end.
- No single-source claims for anything load-bearing — a stat that will anchor a headline or hook needs at least one corroborating source.
- Flag stale data: if the freshest source for a fast-moving topic is more than ~2 years old, say so rather than presenting it as current.
- Flag disagreement: if sources conflict, surface the conflict rather than quietly picking the one that fits the narrative.
- Note paywalled or inaccessible sources so the writer knows to work around them.
- Never fabricate a citation. If a claim can't be sourced, mark it "unverified" and let the writer decide whether to cut it.

## Output format

A research brief:
1. Topic/angle restated in one line
2. Numbered findings, each with: the claim (one line), the source (name + link), one line of context, and a confidence tag (verified / single-source / unverified)
3. Open questions or gaps flagged explicitly
4. Optional: 2–3 candidate stats or quotes worth leading with
